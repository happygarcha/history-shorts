# Post package: Romanov / Anastasia (2026-10-14)

**Title:** She claimed to be the Tsar's lost daughter. DNA answered.

**Description:**
She swore she was Grand Duchess Anastasia. For decades, many believed her.
In 1918 the Russian imperial family was executed at Yekaterinburg, but rumors said one daughter escaped. In 1920 a woman in Berlin, later known as Anna Anderson, claimed to be her. Then a hidden grave was found in 1991, and DNA tests identified the family. Anna Anderson's own DNA did not match the Romanovs; it matched the family of a Polish factory worker.
Sources: Gill et al., Nature Genetics 1994; Coble et al., PLoS ONE 2009.

**Fact-check before posting:**
- Executed at Yekaterinburg in July 1918 (Tsar, Tsarina, five children, plus attendants).
- 1991 grave held 9 skeletons; DNA matched the Tsar and Tsarina.
- Anna Anderson's DNA (tested in 1994) did not match the Romanovs and matched relatives of Franziska Schanzkowska, a Polish factory worker; the last two missing children were identified in 2007-2008.
